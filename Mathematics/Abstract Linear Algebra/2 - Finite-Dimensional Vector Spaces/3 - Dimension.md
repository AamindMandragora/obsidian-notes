All bases of a finite-dimensional vector space have the same length, so we can define the **dimension** of a vector space to be the length of that space's basis. This means that the dimension of $\mathbb F^n$ is $n$. Also, since every subspace of a finite-dimensional vector space is itself finite-dimensional, it must have a dimension that is less than or equal to that of its parent space. This definition of dimension means that any linearly independent list with length equal to the dimension is a basis, which in turn implies that a subspace with the same dimension of the parent space is equal to the parent space.

The dimension of the sum of two spaces is equal to the sum of the dimension of each space minus the dimension of the intersection. This means that the dimension of a direct sum of spaces is simply the sum of dimensions.
