If we have the number of occurrences of some event in a given continuous interval, then we have an **approximate Poisson process** with parameter $\lambda>0$ representing the rate of that event occurring per unit of interval as long as: the numbers of occurrences in nonoverlapping subintervals are independent; the probability of exactly one occurrence in a sufficiently short subinterval of length $h$ is approximately $\lambda h$; and the probability of two or more occurrences in a sufficiently short subinterval is essentially zero. Let $X\sim Poisson(\lambda)$, then $f(x)=\frac{\lambda^xe^{-\lambda}}{x!}$, $E[X]=\lambda$, and $Var[X]=\lambda$.
