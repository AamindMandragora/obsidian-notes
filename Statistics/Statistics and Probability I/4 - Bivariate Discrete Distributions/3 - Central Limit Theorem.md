Let $\set{X_1,\ldots,X_n}$ be a sequence of **independent and identically distributed** (i.i.d) random variables. This means that each variable $X_i$ is independent of each other variable $X_j$, and the mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$ of each $E_k$ is equal. We will create a new sequence $\set{Y_1,\ldots,Y_n}$ where $Y_i=\frac{X_i-\mu}{\sigma}$, which means the mean of $Y_i$ is zero and the variance is one.

We will define the average of all $X_i$ to be $\bar X$. Then, the standardized sample mean $Z_n$ satisfies $$Z_n=\frac{\bar X-\mu}{\sigma\sqrt n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nY_i$$The moment generating function of $Y_i$ equals $M_{Y_i}(t)=E[e^{tY_i}]$. Then, the moment generating function of $Z_n$ satisfies $$M_{Z_n}(t)=E[e^{tZ_n}]=E[e^{\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nY_i}]=E\left[\prod_{i=1}^ne^{\frac{iY_i}{\sqrt n}}\right]$$Since each $Y_i$ is independent and they all sample from the same distribution: $$M_{Z_n}(t)=\prod_{i=1}^nM_{Y_i}(t/\sqrt{n})=(M_Y(t/\sqrt n))^n$$We know $M_Y(0)=1$, $M_Y'(0)=E[Y_i]=0$, and $M_Y''=E[Y_i^2]=1$, so let's try writing the Taylor series of $M_Y(t/\sqrt n)$": $$\begin{align}M_Y(t/\sqrt n)&=M_Y(0)+M_Y'(0)\frac{t}{\sqrt n}+\frac{M_Y''(0)}{2}(t/\sqrt n)^2+o(t^2/n) \\ &=1 + \frac{t^2}{2n}+o\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\end{align}$$Plugging it into the MGF of $Z_n$, we get $$M_{Z_n}(t)=\left(1+\frac{t^2/2}{n}+o\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)^n$$and we can now use the limit formula for $e^k$ to write $M_{Z_n}(t)=e^{t^2/2}$, which is the MGF of the standard normal distribution $N(0, 1)$ as $n$ approaches $\infty$. 

Recall that we defined $\bar X$ to be the sum of all $X_i$, then since $Z_N$ samples from $N(0, 1)$, $S_n\sim N(\mu, \sigma^2/n)$ as $n$ tends to infinity. In practice, this means that if $n\geq 30$, even if the population isn't normally distributed, the sampling distribution of the sample mean is **approximately** normal with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2/n$.
