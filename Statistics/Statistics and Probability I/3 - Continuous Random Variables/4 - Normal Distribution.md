The **normal distribution** is the most important distribution in statistics, fitting many natural phenomena. It's symmetric with a central peak and tapered ends. Approximately 68 / 95 / 99.7 percent of the data falls within 1 / 2 / 3 standard deviations of the mean.

For a random variable $X$ sampling from normal distribution with parameters $\mu$, $\sigma^2$: $$\begin{align}f(x)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}e^{\tfrac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}}\text{, }-\infty<x<\infty \\ E[X] &= \mu \\ Var[X] &=\sigma^2 \end{align}$$To find $P[a<X<b]$, we'd need to evaluate the integral $$\int_a^b\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}e^{\tfrac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}}dx$$and since a closed-form expression for this integral doesn't exist, we'll need numerical integration techniques to solve.

For the normal distribution, $P[X>c]=P[X<-c]$, mean equals median equals mode, and the area is split evenly between positive and negative $X$. By the **Linear Transformation Theorem**, $Y=\alpha X+\beta$ also follows the normal distribution $N(\alpha\mu+\beta, \alpha^2\sigma^2)$. This means that we can convert any normal distribution to the standard $N(0, 1)$ by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation. This conversion ($Z=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma}$) transforms each point in $X$ to its corresponding z-score, the number of standard deviations that point lies above or below the mean of $X$.
