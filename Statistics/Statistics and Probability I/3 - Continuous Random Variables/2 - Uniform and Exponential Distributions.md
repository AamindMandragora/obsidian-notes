A random variable $X$ has a **uniform distribution** if all possible outcomes within a specific interval $[a,b]$ are equally likely, which would make $f(x)=\frac{1}{b-a}$ for $a\leq x\leq b$ and $0$ otherwise. This makes $E[X]=\frac{a+b}{2}$ and $Var[X]=\frac{(b-a)^2}{12}$.

If we have a Poisson process with parameter $\lambda$, we can also count the waiting time $W$ until the first occurrence. Then $W$ follows an **exponential distribution**. For a Poisson distribution, the probability of no occurrences in an interval of length $w$ equals $e^{-\lambda w}$, so $F(w)=P(W\leq w)=1-P(W>w)=1-e^{-\lambda w}$ and $f(w)=F'(w)=\lambda e^{-\lambda w}$. We often choose to use the parameter $\theta =\lambda^{-1}$ to say, for $X$ sampling from an exponential distribution $f(x)=\frac{1}{\theta}e^{-x/\theta}$, $E[X]=\theta$, and $Var[X]=\theta^2$. The derivations for those last two require topics more advanced than we know.

A **memoryless** phenomenon doesn't update its state based on any outside information. The exponential and geometric distributions are memoryless. Discrete memorylessness states that if $X$ is the total number of trials required for the first success, $P[X>m+n\mid X\geq m]=P[X>n]$. Continuous memorylessness states that if $X$ is the total time required for the first success, $P[X>t+s\mid X\geq t]=P[X>s]$.
