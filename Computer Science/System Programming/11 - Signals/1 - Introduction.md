Signals are a convenient way to deliver low-priority information and for users to interact with their programs without using standard input or other methods. They allow a program to respond in some way, like cleaning up, when an event occurs. Programs can even choose to ignore events, but it's hard to effectively use signals due to the way they're handled.

These signals aren't the interrupts generated by the hardware. Those are almost always handled by the kernel as they require higher privilege. Signals are software interrupts generated by the kernel (though they may be in response to a hardware interrupt).
