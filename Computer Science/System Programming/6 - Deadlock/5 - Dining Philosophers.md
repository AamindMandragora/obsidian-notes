Imagine we invite $n$ philosophers to a dinner table with $n$ seats, $n$ plates of food, and $n$ chopsticks in between the plates. Each philosopher alternates between wanting to eat or think, and to eat, the philosopher must pick up the two chopsticks on either side of their plate. We will also assume that each philosopher runs on the same principles and can't do anything that would require recognizing their own self as unique among the philosophers.
# Failed Solutions

If we ask each philosopher to pick up their left chopstick and wait on their right, then there is a high chance of deadlocking inversely proportional to the number of philosophers at the table. Therefore, we need to modify this to break one of the Coffman Conditions. If we try to break `hold-and-wait`, we can make each philosopher put down their left chopstick if the one on the right isn't available. While this solution can't deadlock, if each philosopher moves at exactly the same time, they livelock.
