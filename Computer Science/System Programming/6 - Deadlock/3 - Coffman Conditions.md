We don't see deadlock in an OS as sometimes it prevents some cycle-making processes, but there's still a chance it could happen. The four necessary and sufficient conditions for deadlock are known as the **Coffman Conditions** and are: mutual exclusion, circular wait (cycle in RAG), hold and wait (resources are locked by their process), and no pre-emption (processes can't be forced to give up a resource). The proof that these conditions are necessary and sufficient is pretty simple and is omitted, but does introduce a new problem called **livelock**, which is when the system is stuck in a cycle of states and cannot escape, so while processes do change states, the system doesn't move forward. This makes livelock much harder to detect and, while there are necessary conditions for it, there are no sufficient conditions, which means we must enumerate each of the steps and detect if one of them leads to forward progress in finite steps. For some systems, we can prove bounded waits as well.
